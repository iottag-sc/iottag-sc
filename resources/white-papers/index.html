The Operational Intelligence Framework

How Leading Organisations Create Better Operational Understanding

Executive Summary

Operations are becoming increasingly connected, data-rich and complex.

Workforce systems, fleet technologies, environmental monitoring, production platforms, communications systems and operational technologies generate enormous volumes of information every day.

Yet despite significant investment in technology, many organisations still struggle to answer fundamental operational questions:

What is happening?

Why is it happening?

What conditions are changing?

Where should attention be focused?

  • The challenge is not a lack of information.
  • The challenge is understanding.

Operational Intelligence provides a framework for transforming operational information into actionable insight by helping organisations understand how operational conditions interact across the entire operation.

This paper explores the Operational Intelligence Framework and how organisations can improve safety, productivity and operational performance through better operational understanding.

CHAPTER 2

The Limitations Of Traditional Visibility

Historically, operational technologies have focused on solving specific problems.

Personnel tracking.

Fleet management.

Environmental monitoring.

Production reporting.

Asset tracking.

Each delivers value.

However, each typically operates independently.

This creates fragmented visibility.

Operational teams are forced to review multiple systems to understand what is happening across the operation.

The result is often:

Information overload.

Delayed decision-making.

Operational blind spots.

Missed relationships between operational conditions.

Visibility improves.

Understanding remains limited.

CHAPTER 3

What Is Operational Intelligence?

Operational Intelligence is the ability to understand how operational conditions interact across an environment in real time.

Rather than focusing on individual systems, Operational Intelligence focuses on relationships.

People.

Vehicles.

Equipment.

Environmental conditions.

Production activities.

Operational constraints.

The goal is to create a shared operational understanding that supports better decision-making.

Operational Intelligence helps organisations answer:

What is happening?

Why is it happening?

What factors are contributing?

Who or what is affected?

What requires attention?

CHAPTER 6

From Data To Understanding

For example:

A ventilation fan stops operating
Airflow decreases
Heat levels increase.
Gas concentrations rise.
Personnel remain working within affected areas
Operational exposure increases

Rather than presenting each condition separately, the platform can explain how those conditions are related and why they matter.

This helps organisations move from monitoring information to understanding operational impact.

Traditional systems may present each event separately.

Operational Intelligence identifies the relationship between those events and helps explain why they matter.

This is where understanding begins.

CHAPTER 7

The Role Of AI In Operational Intelligence

As operations become increasingly complex, AI plays an important role in helping organisations interpret information.

AI should not replace operational expertise.

It should augment it.

By analysing relationships between operational conditions, AI can help transform operational information into simple, actionable narratives.

For example:

Current ventilation performance has reduced within Zone 4
Airflow has decreased and environmental conditions continue to trend upward.
Twenty-three personnel remain working within affected areas.
Review ventilation systems and workforce deployment within impacted zones.

This helps operational teams understand what requires attention without needing to interpret multiple systems independently.
